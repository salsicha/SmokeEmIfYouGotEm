# Pacuare Huacas reach: rapid observations (2026-09-29)

Full data: `pacuare_huacas_observations.json` (next to this file).

## How stations were derived

GoRafting river km was converted to OSM chainage as GR km × 1000 + 69,711.6 m. The offset is calibrated on the OSM node "Huacas Abajo" at GR 13.33. The resulting points were projected onto the `pacuare_huacas` centreline. IGN features were projected in CRTM05. Each station carries about ±100 m of uncertainty, because GoRafting does not say which part of a rapid its km refers to. The OSM rapid nodes may come from the same author as GoRafting, so the two sources are probably not independent.

## Why 1750–2250 m looks mild in the cook

The whitewater photographed there is explained by named rapids. The zone holds two named Class III rapids (Upper and Lower Pinball) and the head of Guatemala. The sources point to a wide, shallow, boulder-studded bed:
- the "pinball" name and "read and run" description;
- two mid-river rocks at Lower Pinball;
- a left-bank tributary fan at about 2012 m;
- a low right-bank terrace on the inside of the bend at 1900–2000 m.

The whitewater there is more likely many rock-scale hydraulics than a few big ledges. The IGN contour steps give only ~1.1 % mean slope over 1468–2340 m, against ~1.7–1.8 % in the two Huacas steps. Dense submerged and emergent roughness is the probable missing ingredient, not a larger overall drop.

## Flow context

- **No gauge.** A painted rock at GR 9.67 (river left) is read as green = fine, orange = high but runnable, red = too high.
- **Discharge figures.** One outfitter quotes an average of ~2200 cfs (~62 m³/s). An AI-generated page citing an IMN basin study gives ~88 m³/s mean for the whole basin and ~48 m³/s in January. Neither is verified.
- **What 45 m³/s probably means.** Normal to medium-low: the typical dry-season commercial level. At that level the Huacas rapids stay Class IV and Double Drop is Class III.
- **Flows shown in the media.** Low (Jan 2023, Mar 2022), medium-low (2019), medium/high (18 June 2024), high (Nov 2019 and the Paddler "orange" reading).

## Banks

The gorge has steep to vertical walls, vegetated to the water, with rounded boulder banks and bars. Along the reach:
- **Forest.** Sources describe "first-generation" rainforest, within the Pacuare Protected Zone and the Barbilla corridor (Cabécar Nairi Awari territory).
- **Measured canopy.** No source gives a canopy height or a species list for the gorge. Use a canopy-height raster (Meta/WRI 1 m or ETH 10 m, both CC BY 4.0).
- **IGN relief:**
  - Huacas Falls (700–800 m): river right rises about 80 m within 100 m horizontally.
  - Lower Huacas: the left wall is steep.
  - Pinball: the valley opens.
- **Settlement.** The Cabécar hamlet of Jameikäri lies about 1 km east of the reach end, on river right.
