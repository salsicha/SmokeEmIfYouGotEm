# Zambezi upper gorge (Boiling Pot to below Stairway): observation summary

Full details and sources are in `zambezi_upper_gorge_observations.json`. Stations are on the `zambezi_upper_gorge` centreline.

## Key findings

- **Best low-water analogue.** Esri Wayback holds a WorldView-3 scene from **2023-11-26**. ZRA's Big Tree gauge read 298 m3/s on 21 Nov and 325 m3/s on 29 Nov, so the scene shows about **300-320 m3/s**, close to the 283 m3/s model flow.
  - A WorldView-2 scene from 2021-09-03 (468 m3/s) puts every feature within about 30-50 m of the same stations.
  - A Sept-2019 raft video with on-screen rapid titles confirms the order of the rapids.
- **Sides.** Zambia is always river left and Zimbabwe river right. The supplied centreline hugs the river-left edge of the imaged water in the Second and Third Gorges and the inside of the U-bend.
- **Power-station inflow.** The ZESCO tailrace cascades into the bend from river left at about **2540 m**.
  - Upstream, the model probably carries less than the gauge flow. Our estimate is up to about 110-130 m3/s diverted at full 108 MW; no source gives this number.
  - This inflow is probably the "whitewater at about 2.5 km" seen in photos.
- **Slack stretch.** At about 300 m3/s there is no breaking whitewater from **700 to 1150 m**, only a riffled pool with a sand patch on river left at about 790 m. This matches the photos.
- **Stairway lip is further downstream than Sentinel-2 suggests.** It sits at about **2890-2945 m**, with whitewater running to about 3050 m, not at 2700-2800 m. Only at high water (June 2025) does the whitewater start near 2780 m. A ledge wave at about 2800 m in the approach may explain the Sentinel-2 signal.
- **Naming.** "Between Two Worlds" is Rapid 2, the bridge wave train, in every outfitter source. Guidebooks and kayakers call the same rapid "The Bridge". The stylised map's "#3 Between Two Worlds" is shifted by one.
- **Rapid 6 is probably outside the reach.** Devil's Toilet Bowl most likely lies past the reach end (3468 m), at about 3550-4200 m (low confidence).

## Flow notes

- **Season.** Low-water trips from Rapid 1 run from about August to December. At high water, trips start at Rapid 10/11 or lower, and rafting closes around the April-May peak. The river level in the gorge varies by about 20 m through the year.
- **Low water sharpens the features.** Pool-drop holes, pour-overs and wave trains become distinct. At high water they merge into big wave fields with strong boils and whirlpools.
- **Rapid-specific changes.** Rapid 6 turns from a whirlpool constriction at high water into two waves at low water. On Stairway, the main tongue gets easier as the water rises while the boof gets more retentive.

## Banks and vegetation

- **Walls.** Near-vertical black to dark-grey basalt walls, about 100-120 m high near the bridge (the deck is 128 m above low water), showing horizontal lava-flow banding. A bare, scoured band of basalt and boulders lines the waterline.
- **Slopes and rims.** Talus aprons carry dense mixed woodland. The rims are mopane woodland. In the model season (Aug-Oct) these are leafless grey-brown, with red soil showing on the Zimbabwe rim of the Third Gorge. They turn green after the November rains.
- **Near the falls.** Spray-fed evergreen rainforest (waterberry, figs, mahogany, palms) covers the Zimbabwe strip north of the Second Gorge and the area around the Boiling Pot.
- **Beaches.** Few and small: pale patches at about 790 L, 2000 L, 2850 R and 3170 R, plus rocky landings at the Boiling Pot.
