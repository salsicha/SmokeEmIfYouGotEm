# River Wildlife Reference (sights and procedural calls)

This is a species reference for adding wildlife that a rafter would realistically see or hear on each of the six modelled runs. For each river it picks 4 to 6 species that are iconic and also genuinely likely on that exact section. Each pick has notes for placement, animation, low-poly modelling and procedural call synthesis.

**How to read it**
- **Sourced vs estimated.** Unmarked figures are sourced. *est.* marks our own estimate, read from typical recordings or descriptions where no published number exists. Tune those by ear.
- **"iNat N".** This is the count of verifiable iNaturalist observations within the stated radius, pulled from the API on 2026-10-02. "GBIF N" is the same idea for GBIF, which is mostly eBird data. Treat both as relative abundance and season indicators, not census data.
- **Mixing rule.** Whitewater noise sits mostly below about 1.5 to 2 kHz. Torrent-bird calls put their energy at about 3 to 6 kHz so they carry over it (torrent duck, [Eldridge 1979, *Wildfowl*](https://wildfowl.wwt.org.uk/index.php/wildfowl/article/download/597/597); Himalayan torrent birds, Martens & Geduldig 1990 via [Wikipedia: Dipper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipper)). So in rapids only high calls should survive the mix. Play low calls (roars, croaks, wahoos) in pools and eddies, with long canyon reverb.
- **Flap rate.** Derive it from model size with Pennycuick's formula, f = m^(3/8) g^(1/2) b^(-23/24) S^(-1/3) rho^(-3/8) ([Pennycuick 1996](https://tethys.pnnl.gov/publications/wingbeat-frequency-birds-steady-cruising-flight-new-data-improved-predictions)). Large raptors land at roughly 2 to 3 Hz. Condors and vultures should almost never flap.

## Quick verdicts

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Hippos in Batoka Gorge? | **No, not on the rafted rapids section.** Hippos are abundant above Victoria Falls "but none where the big rapids are located below the Falls"; they "start appearing" only toward the far end of Batoka Gorge on multi-day trips ([SierraRios](https://www.sierrarios.org/GuidedTrips/RaftTripInfo_Zambezi.html)). Also: "in the Batoka gorge there are no hippos and few crocs, but above and below there are plenty" ([Diegel 2024](https://www.tomdiegel.com/2024/01/15/zambezi-river-trip-part-ii/)). AFAR's "two dozen hippos" were seen on the final evening of a 5-day trip, i.e. the lower gorge ([AFAR](https://afar.com/magazine/this-is-africas-wildest-ride)). Marketing copy calling the gorge "hippo infested" is wrong for rapids 1 to 25. |
| Nile crocodiles there? | **Yes, but few and small.** Typical sightings are juveniles about 0.6 m long basking on rocks ([Richard](https://terryjrichard.substack.com/p/zambezi-river-rafting-thrills-with), [Whitewater Guidebook](https://www.whitewaterguidebook.com/africa/zambezi-river-batoka-gorge/)). Occasionally there is a medium one, e.g. below Rapid 14 in Nov to Dec 2021 ([SierraRios](https://www.sierrarios.org/GuidedTrips/RaftTripInfo_Zambezi.html)). |
| Bald eagles on the South Fork American? | **Yes, mainly winter to spring and mainly at the lower Gorge end.** Look near Salmon Falls and the Folsom Lake South Fork arm. iNat 26 within 6 km of Salmon Falls, peaking Mar to May. Only 4 near Chili Bar or Coloma. Rare in mid-summer. |
| Howler monkeys on the Pacuare? | **Yes, but heard more than seen, and more often on the lower run.** iNat 9 within 10 km and 56 within 25 km, in every month. |
| Torrent ducks on the Futaleufú? | **Not documented on the rafted main stem.** Zero iNat records within 50 km. The only eBird/GBIF record near town is from Lago Espolón, a tributary (Apr 2023). They do live in the wider basin: Río Frey, Menéndez and Percy in Argentina, and the Palena. Use them as a rare pair at a tributary mouth only. |
| Chilko, Lava Canyon: bears, eagles, salmon? | **Salmon: yes, they migrate through about mid-Aug to mid-Sep.** They spawn upstream between the lake outlet and Henry's Crossing, not in the canyon. **Bald eagles: yes, all summer.** **Bears:** the famous concentration (more than 80 grizzlies) is at the lake outlet from mid-September. In Lava Canyon in July and August a bear is a rare bonus, and more likely a black bear. GBIF and iNat have zero records inside the canyon itself; that is an observer gap. |

## 1. South Fork American River, CA (Chili Bar to Coloma, Gorge to Salmon Falls)

Habitat is blue oak, gray pine and chaparral, with an alder and willow fringe. Rafting runs April to September.

| Species | Where seen | Behaviour | Look (low-poly) | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bald eagle** | Lower Gorge and Folsom arm. Perches on gray pines and snags over water, or soars 50 to 300 m up. | Long perches, flat-winged soaring, surface fish snatch. Singles or pairs. | 0.7 to 1.0 m long, 1.8 to 2.3 m wingspan. Adult dark brown with white head and tail, yellow bill and feet. Immatures mottled brown for 4 to 5 years. | Weak piping chatter. No scream. |
| **Common merganser** | Eddies and pools. Hens lead duckling strings through riffles (May to Jul). | Swims in line, dives, patters along the surface to take off, flies fast and low along the river. Groups of 1 to 15. | 0.55 to 0.7 m long. Hen grey with rusty crest. Drake white with dark green head. Thin red bill. | Mostly silent; hen croaks "karr". |
| **Turkey vulture** | Over the canyon, 30 to 300 m up. Roosts on snags. | Shallow-V (dihedral) wings, teeters, almost never flaps. Groups of 1 to 10. | 0.65 to 0.8 m long, about 1.75 m wingspan. Black-brown with silver trailing half of the underwing. Small red bare head. | Silent (hiss only). |
| **Red-shouldered hawk** | Bank oaks and sycamores, 5 to 20 m up. Heard more than seen. | Perch-hunts. Flap-flap-glide flight. | 0.43 to 0.61 m long, about 1 m wingspan. Rufous barred breast, checkered wings, black-and-white banded tail. | "kee-aah" series. |
| **Acorn woodpecker** | Oaks and snags on the banks; acorn "granary" trees. | Family groups of 3 to 10. Undulating swoops between trees. Flycatches over the water. | About 0.2 m long. Black back, white face, red cap. White rump and wing patches. | Laughing "waka-waka". |
| **Great blue heron** | Slack edges and gravel bars. Flushes ahead of the raft. | Stands still. Slow deep wingbeats, neck folded. Solitary. | 1.0 to 1.4 m tall, 1.7 to 2.0 m wingspan. Blue-grey, white face, black plume. | Harsh "fraaank". |

**Evidence** (iNat within 12 km of Coloma unless noted)
- **Bald eagle.** As in the verdict table. Supporting: Sacramento Audubon says bald eagles are usual in winter at Folsom Lake Peninsula, between the forks ([SAS](https://www.sacramentoaudubon.org/sas-birding-locations/folsom-lake-peninsula)). A nesting pair at nearby Lake Natoma can be watched Oct to Jul ([ABC10](https://abc10.com/article/news/local/bald-eagles-frequent-folsom-lake-natoma/103-7bddc427-363c-4df2-912f-702a61d8a9a7)).
- **Common merganser:** iNat 47, Mar to Sep. **Turkey vulture:** iNat 84, all year. **Acorn woodpecker:** iNat 87. **Great blue heron:** iNat 19. **Red-shouldered hawk:** iNat 533 within 25 km.
- **Other wildlife.** Outfitter copy mentions river otters, osprey and golden eagles ([AdvCollective](https://app.advcollective.com/attraction/riding-the-gold-country-rapids-rafting-the-south-fork-american-river), [gorafting](https://gorafting.com/united-states/california/south-fork-american/)). River otter has iNat 12.
- **Weakly supported.** Osprey has only iNat 4. Canyon wren has 0 within 12 km and 3 within 25 km. Keep the canyon wren for the Grand Canyon.

**Acoustics**
- **Bald eagle.** Cornell describes the calls as "weak", a series of high whistling or piping notes ([All About Birds](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Bald_Eagle/sounds)). The movie "eagle scream" is a dubbed red-tailed hawk ([Maine Public](https://www.mainepublic.org/2025-07-03/bald-eagle-sound-hollywood-red-tailed-hawk)).
  - *Chatter:* 3 to 4 introductory notes with gaps under 1 s, then 6 to 9 rapid, descending piping notes. Overall **3 to 5 kHz**.
  - *Peal:* about 0.5 s gull-like notes, slightly rising, repeated 3 to 5 times, ending in a voice break. Source: [Birds of the World](https://birdsoftheworld.org/bow/species/baleag/cur/sounds), quoted from a search snippet because the page is paywalled.
  - *est.:* 8 to 12 notes per second, near-pure tones. Carries 200 to 400 m. Pairs often duet.
- **Red-shouldered hawk.** A 2-syllable "kee-aah", usually repeated **5 to 12 times**. Females are lower ([BirdForum](https://birdforum.net/opus/Red_Shouldered_Hawk)).
  - *est.:* 0.3 to 0.5 s downslurred notes, about 1.5 to 3 kHz, about 1 per second. Carries about 1 km. Peaks Feb to May.
  - Steller's jays mimic it ([Humboldt thesis](https://digitalcommons.humboldt.edu/etd/99)).
- **Acorn woodpecker.** Nasal "waka-waka" and "ja-cob" series, plus a scratchy "krrrit-kut" ([Birdzilla](https://www.birdzilla.com/birds/acorn-woodpecker/)).
  - *est.:* units about 0.12 s, in bursts of 3 to 6, about 1 to 2 kHz, rich harmonics. Several birds overlap.
- **Common merganser.** Hoarse "gruk", sometimes in a series. Low grunts to ducklings ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_merganser)).
  - *est.:* 0.15 to 0.3 s noisy croaks at 0.5 to 2 kHz.
- **Great blue heron.** Guttural "fraaank" alarm and flight call ([BirdNote](https://birdnote.org/show/regal-great-blue-heron)).
  - *est.:* 1 to 4 croaks of 0.3 to 0.8 s each, broadband 0.3 to 1.5 kHz.
- **Turkey vulture.** It has no syrinx and only hisses or grunts ([BirdWeb](https://birdweb.org/Birdweb/bird/turkey_vulture)).

**Ambience**
- **Sierran treefrog chorus.** iNat 191, Feb to Jul. A two-part "krek-ek" with a rising second syllable, given day and night in building choruses. This frog group is Hollywood's stock night-frog sound ([CaliforniaHerps](https://www.californiaherps.com/frogs/pages/p.regilla.sounds.html)).
  - *est.:* 0.2 to 0.3 s calls, about 2 to 2.5 kHz, about 1 per second per frog.
- **California quail.** iNat 293 within 25 km. A three-note "chi-CA-go" from the brush.
- **Cicadas.** *Okanagana* cicadas buzz in July heat.

## 2. Colorado River, Grand Canyon, Hance Rapid (RM 76.5)

Hance marks the entry to the Upper Granite Gorge, with dark schist walls ([Western River](https://www.westernriver.com/grand-canyon-vacation/mile-by-mile-76-hance-rapid)). The South Rim (Grandview and Moran points) is about 1.4 km directly above.

| Species | Where seen | Behaviour | Look | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **California condor** | Rims and cliffs, 100 to 1,000 m above the river. Occasionally on ledges. | Soars with near-zero flapping. Long glides, slow circles, splayed "finger" primaries. 1 to 5 birds. | 1.1 to 1.4 m long, 2.7 to 2.9 m wingspan. Black with white underwing triangles. Adults have a bare orange-pink head. Numbered wing tags. | Silent. |
| **Desert bighorn** | Talus, ledges and beaches at the waterline, often drinking. 20 to 200 m away. | Picks across cliffs, stands and watches. Herds of up to 20. Rams butt heads in the Jul to Oct rut. | Ram up to about 113 kg, about 0.9 m at the shoulder. Tan-brown with white muzzle, rump and belly. Ram has massive curled horns; ewe has short spikes. | Horn clash; bleats. |
| **Common raven** | Rims, beaches and camps. More common up high than at the river. | Pairs tumble and barrel-roll. Struts on beaches. | About 0.6 m long, about 1.2 m wingspan. Glossy black, wedge tail, shaggy throat. | Deep croaks and knocks. |
| **Canyon wren** | Cliffs and boulders at river level. Heard, rarely seen. | Creeps on vertical rock and bobs. Solitary. | 14 to 15 cm. Chestnut with speckles, bright white throat, long thin bill. | Descending whistle cascade, the signature canyon sound. |
| **Great blue heron** | Eddies and beaches at the tails of rapids. | Still-hunts, then flushes downstream. | As on the South Fork. | "Fraaank". |

**Evidence**
- **California condor.** More than 70 birds range over northern Arizona and southern Utah. Rafters see them mainly at Navajo Bridge and below the South Rim ([CRATE](https://crateinc.com/?p=291), [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/grca/blogs/condor-success-story.htm)). iNat has 14 within 6 km of Hance and 419 within 25 km, Mar to Oct with a peak May to Aug.
- **Desert bighorn.** NPS describes one of the largest naturally persisting desert bighorn populations, concentrated in the river corridor ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/nature/bighorn-sheep.htm)). Herds of up to 20 are seen along the river ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/desert-bighorn-sheep.htm)). iNat 22 within 6 km.
- **Canyon wren.** "Very common all along the river… heard resonating from the cliffs despite the fact that they were rarely seen" ([Schell, UC Davis river-trip report](https://cwseducation.ucdavis.edu/class/31/birds-grand-canyon-march)).
- **Raven.** The same report found ravens common near the rim, uncommon at the river except at mealtimes. It also notes dippers only on tributaries, and turkey vultures only below RM 150.
- **Great blue heron.** A year-round resident ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/nature/riparian-birds.htm)).

**Acoustics**
- **California condor.** No syrinx, so only hisses and grunts audible at a few metres ([Kern Audubon](https://www.kernaudubonsociety.org/bird-of-the-week-california-condor/)). Use silence, or a broadband wing "whoosh" below 1 kHz on close passes (*est.*).
- **Desert bighorn.** Horn clashes carry about 1.6 km ([Nebraskaland](https://digital.outdoornebraska.gov/nebraskaland-magazine/clash-of-the-bighorns)).
  - *est.:* a sharp broadband crack under 20 ms with a 0.2 to 0.5 s wall echo, every 10 to 60 s during the rut.
  - Rams snort before charging.
  - Ewes and lambs give "maa" bleats (*est.* 0.3 to 0.6 s, F0 300 to 600 Hz). Add rockfall clatter under the hooves.
- **Common raven.**
  - Defensive calls average **0.14 s**, F0 about **450 Hz**, strongly frequency-modulated ([Szipl et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5740903/)).
  - Food "haa" calls average F0 about **600 to 830 Hz** by age and sex ([Boeckle et al. 2018](https://frontiersinzoology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12983-018-0255-z)).
  - *est.:* croaks 0.2 to 0.5 s in runs of 2 to 5, harmonics to about 4 kHz, plus hollow knocks. Audible wing creak up close.
- **Canyon wren.** A descending cascade of clear notes, sometimes ending in up to 3 to 7 harsher broadband notes ([Benedict et al. 2013](https://academic.oup.com/condor/article/115/4/874/5152841)).
  - Song lasts **2.7 to 3.2 s**. Each male has about 5 song types and sings about 4.6 songs per bout. Repeats of the same type come about 22 s apart; type switches about 79 s apart.
  - The song is about "ten or so" notes, speeding up as they descend ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon_wren)).
  - *est.:* 10 to 20 pure downslurred whistles of 50 to 100 ms each, stepping down from about 5 to 6 kHz to about 2 kHz. 1 to 2 s canyon reverb.

**Ambience**
- **Canyon treefrog.** iNat 115, peak Apr to Jun, dusk and night at side creeks. A loud rattling series of short trills "from inside a tin can" ([Reptiles of AZ](https://reptilesofaz.org/turtle-amphibs-subpages/h-h-arenicolor)), or like distant bleating sheep ([DesertUSA](https://www.desertusa.com/mag06/jun/treefrog.html)).
  - *est.:* 0.5 to 2 s pulsed trills, 1.5 to 2.5 kHz.
- **Apache cicada** (*Diceroprocta apache*). iNat 16, Jun to Aug, in riparian willow ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1015242)). Song "continuous, even toned and moderately shrill" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diceroprocta_apache)).
  - *est.:* a steady buzz at 5 to 9 kHz that swells with heat.

## 3. Pacuare River, Costa Rica (Tres Equis / Upper Huacas to Siquirres)

Caribbean-slope rainforest gorge. iNat counts are for 10 km / 25 km radii around 10.02 N, 83.56 W.

| Species | Where seen | Behaviour | Look | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mantled howler monkey** | Bank canopy 15 to 40 m up, 20 to 80 m away. Lower run. | Slow four-limbed climbing with the tail anchored. Rests most of the day. Troops of 10 to 20. | Body 48 to 68 cm, tail 55 to 66 cm, 3 to 10 kg. Black with a golden-brown flank "mantle". | Roaring bouts. |
| **Montezuma oropendola** | Colony trees 12 to 30 m high at banks and clearings, with hanging nests. Crosses the gorge. | Male bow display: tips forward, wings lifted. Heavy flapping commutes. | Male 50 cm, female 38 cm. Chestnut body, black head, yellow tail, blue cheek, pink wattle, black bill with orange tip. Woven nests 0.6 to 1.8 m long, about 30 per tree. | Gurgle-and-swoop song. |
| **Toucans** (keel-billed; yellow-throated) | Emergent trees on the rim. Fly across the river overhead. | Undulating flight: 6 to 10 flaps then a glide. Groups of 6 to 12. Bill-clacking. | 42 to 61 cm, bill 12 to 20 cm. Black with yellow bib and red undertail. Keel-bill: green, orange and red bill. Yellow-throated: yellow and chestnut bill. | Keel-billed: frog-like croak. Yellow-throated: yelping "Dios te dé". |
| **Sunbittern** | Bank rocks and gravel bars, 5 to 20 m away. | Slow horizontal walk with sudden neck strikes. Spreads its wings to flash eyespots. Low flight over the water. | 43 to 48 cm. Finely barred grey, brown and black. Wings show red, yellow and black eyespots. | Long "wuuuu" whistle. |
| **Ringed kingfisher** | Branches 2 to 10 m over the water. Flies ahead of the raft 1 to 3 m above the surface. | Long perches, straight strong flight along the channel. | About 40 cm long, about 0.64 m wingspan. Blue-grey, shaggy crest, white collar. Male rufous belly. | Loud rattle. |
| **Fasciated tiger-heron** (visual) | Midstream boulders right in the rapids. | Motionless, solitary. | 58 to 66 cm. Slate with fine buff barring, heavy bill. | Nearly silent. |

**Evidence**
- **Mantled howler.** iNat 9 / 56, every month. Outfitters: howlers "holler from the canopy" ([islands.com](https://www.islands.com/1691792/unforgettable-rapids-wildlife-awaits-costa-rica-underrated-adventure-destination-pacuare-river)). Wikipedia places them on the lower sections ([Pacuare River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacuare_River)).
- **Montezuma oropendola.** iNat 36 / 386. Its colonies are a [Pacuare Lodge](https://www.pacuarelodge.com/birdwatching/) highlight. Breeds Jan to May ([ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Psarocolius_montezuma)).
- **Toucans.** Keel-billed iNat 53 / 273 and yellow-throated 33 / 74, so keel-billed is actually the more recorded. Outfitters name the chestnut-mandibled form ([costarica.org](https://costarica.org/rivers/pacuare/)).
- **Sunbittern.** iNat 14 / 192, including records photographed from rafts on the Pacuare ([1](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/222633184), [2](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/221646656)).
- **Ringed kingfisher:** iNat 4 / 44, including at Rios Tropicales lodge. **Tiger-heron:** iNat 12 / 36, standing on midstream boulders ([BirdForum](https://www.birdforum.net/opus/Fasciated_Tiger_Heron)).
- **Animation-only extras:** sloths, white-faced capuchins and blue morphos.
- **Rare flyover:** the critically endangered great green macaw (iNat 11 / 47, peaking in October). Scarlet macaw is essentially absent (0 / 4).

**Acoustics**
- **Mantled howler.** All figures from [Bergman et al. 2016](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/cortes-ortiz-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/568/2019/06/Bergman-et-al.-2016.pdf), *A. palliata*:
  - Each roar lasts **2.2 s** and has about 6 syllables that alternate breathing in and out. Loudness peaks at 73% of the roar.
  - F0 is **112 Hz**. Formants start at 413 Hz and are spaced 624 Hz apart, giving about 413, 1037, 1661, 2285 and 2909 Hz. The loudest band is about 500 Hz.
  - The sound is rough: harmonics-to-noise ratio 7.9 dB.
  - A bout lasts about 9 min and is about half silence, with about 2 roars per minute plus grunting barks.
  - Groups call together at sunrise and carry over 2 km ([Johnson et al. 2023](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10517147)). They also howl at thunder and other loud noise.
  - *Synthesis:* a noisy 112 Hz source through a formant filter bank.
- **Montezuma oropendola** ([Mennill](https://web2.uwindsor.ca/courses/biology/dmennill/Mexico/MOOR.html)):
  - *Song* (0.75 to 2 s): quiet bubbly notes rising from 250 to 900 Hz, then overtone-rich upslurs climbing above 8 kHz. It ends on a loud, long downslurred note.
  - *Contact call:* 0.25 s, sweeping 1500 to 500 Hz, about every 1.7 s.
  - The male's bow is synced to the loudest note ([Animal Behaviour 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/am/pii/S0003347218301155)).
- **Toucans.**
  - Keel-billed: a frog-like "krrrk" repeated steadily, audible to about 0.8 km ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keel-billed_toucan)). *est.:* 0.2 to 0.4 s pulse trains at 30 to 50 pulses/s, 0.5 to 2 kHz, about 1 per second.
  - Yellow-throated: a loud first note, then shorter doubled falling notes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow-throated_toucan)). *est.:* notes 0.1 to 0.3 s, phrase 1 to 1.5 s, F0 about 0.8 to 1.2 kHz, every 3 to 10 s, morning and evening.
- **Sunbittern.** A high "wuuuu" whistle, often given twice, plus a "kak-kak" alarm. *est.:* 1.5 to 2.5 s, 1.5 to 3 kHz, flat or slightly rising.
- **Ringed kingfisher.** A rattle lower than the belted kingfisher's, plus "klek" notes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringed_kingfisher)). *est.:* 1 to 3 s at 8 to 12 clicks/s, 2 to 5 kHz, given when flushed. It cuts through rapids noise.
- **Fasciated tiger-heron.** Rare low rising notes ending in a cow-like "moo" ([Hornero 2015](https://bibliotecadigital.exactas.uba.ar/download/hornero/hornero_v030_n01_p013.pdf)). *est.:* 200 to 500 Hz, about 1 s.

**Ambience**
- **Emerald cicada** (*Zammara smaragdina*, iNat 48 / 88, May to Sep). A pulsing broadband chorus at **2.7 to 6.6 kHz** that starts around **08:40 to 10:40**, so it overlaps a day trip. Birds call less once it starts ([Hart et al. 2015](https://academic.oup.com/beheco/article/26/3/839/234674)).
- **Strawberry poison-dart frog.** iNat 240 / 489, the most-recorded animal here, calling by day. Chirp bouts of 5 to 32 s at 5 to 9 notes/s ([ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Oophaga_pumilio/)). It goes quiet when cicadas sing ([Wong et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2953814)).
  - *est.:* about 2 to 4 kHz. Play only at calm stops.
- Glass frogs and red-eyed treefrogs call at night only, so they suit lodge scenes.

## 4. Futaleufú River, Chile (Terminator / Bridge-to-Bridge)

Glacial turquoise river through Nothofagus and bamboo forest. Peak season is Dec to Mar. Counts are GBIF records in a box of about 33 by 37 km around town.

| Species | Where seen | Behaviour | Look | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Andean condor** | Valley rims and cliffs, 100 to 1,000 m up. | Flaps about 1% of flight time. Endless soaring. Groups of 1 to 7, median 2. | Wingspan up to 3.3 m, 10 to 15 kg. Black with white ruff and white wing panels. Males have a red comb. | Silent. |
| **Ringed kingfisher** | Branches over the water; low runs ahead of the raft. | As on the Pacuare. | As on the Pacuare. | Harsh rattle. |
| **Black-faced ibis** (bandurria) | Flocks flying over the open, pasture-lined reaches; roosts on dead trees and cliffs. | Fast flaps alternating with glides. Calls with the bill pointed skyward. | 71 to 76 cm. Buff neck, black bare face, grey back, long decurved black bill, red legs. | Metallic trumpeting honks. |
| **Southern lapwing** (queltehue) | Gravel bars and riverside pasture. | Mobs intruders. Stiff wingbeats. Pairs. | 32 to 38 cm. Thin crest, black face and breast, bronze shoulders, white wing bar. | Strident "keh-keh". |
| **Chucao tapaculo** | Bamboo and forest floor at the bank. Heard, rarely seen. | Hops with tail cocked. | 19 cm. Dark brown above, rufous face and breast, barred belly. | Loud "chu-chu-chu-cao". |
| **Torrent duck** (rare) | A pair on a boulder at a tributary mouth or side-creek eddy. | Dives up to about 20 s. Swims upstream through eddies. Hops onto rocks. Short low flights. Territory about 0.6 km of river. | 43 to 46 cm, about 0.4 kg. Male: white head with black stripes, red bill. Female: grey above, orange-rufous below. | Male "wheet" whistle. |

**Evidence**
- **Torrent duck.** As in the verdict table ([iNat API](https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/observations?taxon_name=Merganetta%20armata&lat=-43.17&lng=-71.85&radius=50&verifiable=true&per_page=0); [GBIF Espolón record](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4728108006)). Futaleufú Riverkeeper lists it for the sub-basin without naming reaches ([Libro Futa](https://futaleufuriverkeeper.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Libro-Futa.pdf)).
  - Likely reason: the main stem is huge, with a mean flow of about 459 m³/s. Torrent ducks hold territories on smaller, steep rivers ([Pernollet et al. 2013](https://complete.bioone.org/journals/waterbirds/volume-36/issue-3/063.036.0306/Habitat-Selection-by-Torrent-Ducks-Merganetta-armata-armata-in-Central/10.1675/063.036.0306.full)).
- **Other species, GBIF records:**
  - Chucao: 281, resident.
  - Black-faced ibis: 214. A summer migrant, peaking Oct to Feb and absent May to Jun.
  - Southern lapwing: 203.
  - Andean condor: 67, all year.
  - Ringed kingfisher: 61, 48 of them Dec to Mar.
- Condor, kingfisher and cormorant records include localities on the river itself, such as "Río Futaleufú–Las Escalas". An outfitter listing mentions condors watching from the peaks.
- **Skip:** southern river otter (huillín; 0 records within 40 km) and chimango caracara.

**Acoustics**
- **Torrent duck** ([Eldridge 1979, *Wildfowl*](https://wildfowl.wwt.org.uk/index.php/wildfowl/article/download/597/597); the figures come from the search index because the PDF now returns 404):
  - Male: a clear "wheet" whistle. Female: a harsh "queech".
  - Both sexes put their energy at **3 to 5 kHz**, about 4 to 5 kHz when territorial. River noise sits at 0 to 1.5 kHz, so the male carries over rapids and the female is lost.
  - *est.:* male notes 0.2 to 0.4 s, rising about 3.5 to 4.5 kHz, every 1 to 3 s.
- **Andean condor.** No syrinx, so hisses and grunts only ([LA Zoo](https://lazoo.org/explore-your-zoo/our-animals/birds/andean-condor/)). Do not synthesize a call. Flaps only about 1% of flight time ([Williams et al. 2020](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/07/200714111730.htm)).
- **Black-faced ibis.** "Loud trumpeting cries" that carry far ([eBird](https://ebird.org/species/bkfibi1/CL)).
  - *est.:* 0.15 to 0.3 s metallic honks, F0 0.8 to 1.5 kHz, in series of 2 to 6 at 2 to 4 per second. Mostly on morning and evening flights.
- **Southern lapwing.** Harmonic-rich notes with energy from 1.1 to 10 kHz. About 11% of calls are given at night ([Jahn et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5235375/)).
  - *est.:* "keh" notes of 80 to 150 ms at 4 to 8 per second, F0 2 to 3 kHz. Escalates when the bird mobs.
- **Chucao tapaculo.** Soft churrs bracketing 4 to 5 loud, clear, higher notes, "chuu, chu-chu-chu-caou" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chucao_tapaculo)).
  - *est.:* 1.5 to 2.5 s, main notes about 1.5 to 3 kHz, every 10 to 30 s.
- **Ringed kingfisher.** As on the Pacuare.

**Ambience**
- **Coliguacho horse-fly** (*Osca lata*). Active by rivers from mid-Dec to late Jan with a loud buzz ([El Dínamo](https://www.eldinamo.cl/buen-dato/2023/12/20/que-es-el-coliguacho-el-insecto-que-se-toma-el-verano-en-chile/)).
  - *est.:* a 150 to 250 Hz harmonic buzz with Doppler fly-bys around the paddlers.
- **Austral parakeet flocks.** Nasal "grrreh" series ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austral_parakeet)).
  - *est.:* 100 to 200 ms notes, 1.5 to 5 kHz.
- **Magellanic woodpecker.** A double-knock drum ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellanic_woodpecker)).

## 5. Chilko River, BC (Lava Canyon, Bidwell Rapids)

Lava Canyon (51.883 N, 123.901 W) starts about 35 km below Chilko Lake. Its walls are columnar basalt, set in dry Douglas-fir and lodgepole pine. Bidwell Rapids is the Class V entrance, followed by about 14 km of Class IV. OARS launches run 20 Jun to 29 Aug ([OARS](https://oars.com/?p=46113)).

| Species | Where seen | Behaviour | Look | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sockeye salmon** | Bunched in surging eddies along the cliff walls; leaping at ledges. | Groups of 10 to 200 in eddies 0.3 to 1.5 m deep. Leaps 0.3 to 1 m (est.). | 46 to 61 cm. Reddening body, green head; males get a hump and hooked jaw. | Splashes. |
| **Grizzly / black bear** | Gravel bar or bench, 30 to 150 m away. Rare in Jul and Aug. | Slow walk, rises on hind legs to sniff, wades eddy edges. Solo or sow with 1 to 3 cubs. | Grizzly 100 to 170 kg, shoulder hump, dished face, brown to blonde. Black bear 63 to 93 kg, straight face, bigger ears. | Mostly silent. |
| **Bald eagle** | Dead snags and basalt rim tops 20 to 80 m above the river; soaring. | Perch and soar. Singles or pairs in summer. | As on the South Fork. | Piping chatter. |
| **Osprey** | Calmer pools above and below the canyon. | Hovers 10 to 40 m up, then plunges feet-first. Wings bent in an "M". | 50 to 66 cm long, 1.3 to 1.8 m wingspan. Brown above, white below, dark eye mask. | Whistled "cheep" series. |
| **Common merganser** | Hen with 5 to 15 young running rapids or resting on eddy rocks. | Young ride on her back. Broods merge into crèches. | As on the South Fork; males in drab eclipse plumage in summer. | Mostly silent. |
| **Common raven** | Basalt rims and columns. | Pairs tumble along the rims. | As in the Grand Canyon. | Croaks. |

**Evidence**
- **Sockeye.** The Chilko run is one of the largest Fraser sockeye runs. Fish spawn in "a 6-mile section just below" the lake ([SierraRios](https://www.sierrarios.org/Articles/TasekoChilcotin_AW_Sep2010.pdf)), so they only pass through Lava Canyon.
  - 2025 sonar counts: 186k fish by 29 Aug, 1.51M by 9 Sep, about 2.38M by 24 Sep ([PSC](https://www.psc.org/download/723/2025/19375/september-05-2025-2.pdf)). Canyon passage is therefore about mid-Aug to mid-Sep, overlapping late-August trips.
  - Rafters see salmon "bunched up in the surging eddies along the cliff walls" ([Nahanni](https://nahanni.com/?p=215)).
  - Context: the July 2024 Chilcotin landslide near Farwell Canyon cut the 2024 Chilko return by an estimated record 50% ([Live Science](https://livescience.com/planet-earth/rivers-oceans/earth-from-space-massive-landslide-dams-canadian-river-trapping-endangered-fish-on-the-wrong-side)).
- **Bears.** More than 80 grizzlies gather at the lake outlet from mid-September ([OARS Bear Camp](https://www.oars.com/adventures/bear-camp-at-chilko-lake/)). iNat has 25 grizzly records within 40 km, almost all at the outlet in Sep and Oct.
  - Outfitters claim bears generically, "foraging along the shores or fishing for salmon in the rapids" ([Nahanni](https://nahanni.com/blog/rafting-in-the-wild-exploring-the-captivating-fauna-and-flora-of-the-taseko-chilko-and-chilcotin-rivers/)).
- **Corridor birds, GBIF records:**
  - Bald eagle: 53 (Jun to Oct, surging at the outlet when the salmon die).
  - Osprey: 19.
  - Merganser: 46.
  - Raven: 60.
- **Not in Lava Canyon.** California bighorn sheep live at Farwell Canyon and the Junction Sheep Range, not in Lava Canyon. Harlequin duck has zero records. American dipper has only 5, at the outlet.

**Acoustics**
- **Sockeye** (all *est.*): leap splashes are broadband 0.2 to 8 kHz bursts of 0.1 to 0.4 s; tail slaps in shallows 50 to 150 ms. Only audible in eddies, not in the rapids.
- **Bears.** Mostly silent. Huffing, jaw-popping and low growls are stress or warning signals; cubs make a mewling sound ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/bears/sounds.htm), [bear.org](https://bear.org/?p=236)).
  - *est.:* huffs are 2 to 5 breath bursts of 0.2 to 0.5 s; jaw-pops are 5 to 20 ms clicks; growl F0 80 to 200 Hz.
  - Use only within about 30 m on calm water.
- **Bald eagle.** As on the South Fork.
- **Osprey.** Short, clear whistles, "cheep" or "yewk", given in series that rise then fall ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/cabr/learn/nature/osprey.htm)).
  - *est.:* 0.1 to 0.2 s notes, 2 to 4 kHz, 2 to 4 per second.
- **Raven.** As in the Grand Canyon.

**Ambience**
- **Swainson's thrush.** iNat 19. An upward-spiralling flutey song at dawn and dusk.
  - *est.:* 1.5 to 2 s phrases, rising from about 1.5 to 5 kHz.
- **Belted kingfisher.** iNat 36. A dry rattle along the banks.
- Wind in the pines.

## 6. Zambezi, Batoka Gorge (Boiling Pot to Mukuni Beach; upper gorge to Stairway to Heaven)

Black basalt walls 100 to 250 m high with sparse vegetation. Peak rafting is the low-water season, Aug to Dec. iNat counts are for a gorge-only box (18.00 to 17.935 S, 25.86 to 26.00 E) and a wider box that includes the falls and town.

| Species | Where seen | Behaviour | Look | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chacma baboon** | Troops on gorge ledges, scree and bank rocks; they sleep on the cliffs. | Troops of 20 to 60 (est.). Quadrupedal scrambling, sitting, grooming, bark-offs. | Male 21 to 45 kg, female about 15 kg. Grey-brown; the local form is paler with grey feet. Long dog-like muzzle. | "Wa-hoo" barks. |
| **Verreaux's (black) eagle** | Cliff tops 100 to 300 m above the river. | Long soaring on stretched wings; pendulum display flights. Pairs hunt rock hyrax. | 80 to 90 cm long, about 2.0 m wingspan. All black with a white V on the back, white rump and white primary windows. Yellow feet. | Usually silent. |
| **Rock pratincole** | Perched on midstream rocks in and between rapids. | Rests quietly by day; hawks insects over the water at dawn and dusk. Loose groups. | 17 to 20 cm, about 0.4 m wingspan (est.). Dark grey-brown, white collar stripe, coral-red legs. | Thin "kek" and whistles. |
| **Nile crocodile (juvenile)** | Basking on rocks at the waterline, then slipping in. | Lies still with jaws open from 09:00 to 16:00. Silent slide into the water. Singles. | 0.6 to 1.2 m, occasionally about 2 m. Grey-olive with dark bands and a pale belly. | Rarely heard. |
| **African fish eagle** (occasional) | Single bird in the lower-gorge pools (rapids 19 to 25), or a distant call. | Perch-hunts and swoops for fish. Duets with head thrown back. | 63 to 76 cm long, 1.8 to 2.4 m wingspan. White head, breast and tail; chestnut belly; black wings. | "Weee-ah, kyow-kyow-kyow". |
| **Trumpeter hornbill** | Riverine forest at the falls end and the upper gorge (Boiling Pot). | Groups of 3 to 5. Flaps and glides between trees. | 50 to 55 cm. Black with white belly and rump; big grey casque. | Wailing "crying baby". |

**Evidence**
- **Hippo and croc verdict:** see Quick verdicts.
- **Cliff raptors.** A 2013 to 2014 survey of the first 40 km below the falls found at least 4 Verreaux's eagle pairs, 6 peregrine, 3 lanner, 5 augur buzzard and 7 black stork pairs. It found **no Taita falcons**, despite more than 100 hours of observation at former sites ([J. Raptor Res. 2019](https://bioone.org/journals/journal-of-raptor-research/volume-53/issue-1/JRR-18-36/Status-of-the-Taita-Falcon-Falco-fasciinucha-and-Other-Cliff/10.3356/JRR-18-36.full)).
- **Rock pratincole.** A rafting survey counted 103 ([BirdLife Zimbabwe](https://www.birdlifezimbabwe.org/z012-bakota-gorge/)). Densities are 2.2 to 7 birds/km in the gorges. The birds arrive in August as rocks become exposed and leave by January ([Brown 2018](https://nje.org.na/index.php/nje/article/download/volume2-brown/24/323)).
- **Raft-trip lists.** Baboons, klipspringers, vervets, eagles and buzzards ([CheckYeti](https://www.checkyeti.com/en/d/1897/14163)). Black eagle, augur buzzard, black stork, kingfishers and red-winged starling are "normally seen" ([kloosterman](https://www.kloosterman.be/my-interests/rafting/zambezi-rafting/)).
- **iNat, gorge box vs wider box:**

  | Species | Gorge box | Wider box | Note |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Baboon | 8 | 326 | |
  | Verreaux's eagle | 3 | 9 | |
  | Trumpeter hornbill | 1 | 190 | |
  | Fish eagle | 0 | 10 | All 10 at or above the falls |

- The [Batoka checklist](https://www.exoticbirding.com/zambia/batoka-gorge/checklist.html) includes fish eagle and trumpeter hornbill. Fish eagles show up mainly in lower-gorge multi-day accounts ([Cascade Designs](https://cascadedesigns.com/blogs/thermarest-stories/whitewater-rafting-zambezi-river-africa)).

**Acoustics**
- **Chacma baboon "wahoo"** ([Fischer et al. 2002, JASA](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/AIP%20Publishing/Fischer_Acoustic_JAcoustSocAm_2002_1555972.pdf)):
  - Structure: a loud "wa" bark, a short gap, then a quieter "hoo" that may be dropped. It carries more than 1 km.
  - Adult male F0 by context, with the "wa" duration:

    | Context | "wa" F0 | "wa" duration | Calls per minute |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Contest | 312 Hz | 351 ms | about 30 |
    | Contact | 297 Hz | 472 ms | |
    | Alarm | 329 Hz | 322 ms | 4.3 |

  - "Hoo" lasts 116 to 214 ms. Peak frequency is about 740 Hz. Alarm calls are noisier, and baboons give them at lions, leopards and crocodiles.
  - Female barks are single-syllable and higher.
- **African fish eagle.** "Weee-ah, kyo-kyo-kyo" with the head thrown back, usually as a duet. The female starts and has the deeper voice; the male is shriller ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_fish_eagle)). Mostly at dawn and dusk.
  - *est.:* the "weee" rises to about 1.8 to 2.5 kHz then falls, lasting 0.6 to 0.9 s. Then 3 to 5 "kyow" notes of 0.15 to 0.2 s at about 4 per second, around 1.2 to 1.8 kHz.
  - The second bird comes in 0.2 to 0.5 s later, about 10 to 20% lower. Long canyon echo.
- **Verreaux's eagle.** Usually silent. Gives a melodious "keee-up" or harsh yelp in display ([oiseaux-birds](https://www.oiseaux-birds.com/card-verreaux-eagle.html)).
  - *est.:* 1.5 to 3 kHz, about 0.4 s, rare.
- **Rock pratincole.** A faint whistled contact call and musical purring; noisy when aggressive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_pratincole)).
  - *est.:* 3 to 5 kHz "kek" notes of 40 to 80 ms at 5 to 8 per second. This pitch survives rapids noise.
- **Juvenile Nile crocodile** ([Chabert et al. 2015](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4616036/fullTextXML)):
  - Handled juveniles give a harmonic, downward-sweeping call, about 160 ms long.
  - 20 to 40 cm animals: mean F0 456 Hz, sweeping 596 to 318 Hz.
  - 80 to 100 cm animals: F0 389 Hz.
  - *est.:* larger animals mainly hiss, broadband 0.2 to 6 kHz for 0.5 to 1.5 s. In practice play only the water slide and splash.
- **Trumpeter hornbill.** A loud nasal wail, "nhaa nhaa ha-ha-ha", like a crying baby; it brays in flight ([IUCN HSG](https://iucnhornbills.org/trumpeter-hornbill/)).
  - *est.:* F0 0.6 to 1.2 kHz with rich harmonics, notes 0.3 to 0.8 s, in series of 3 to 8.

**Ambience**
- **Red-winged starling.** Cliff-nesting flocks with a whistled "cher-leeeoo" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-winged_starling)). This is the most characteristic gorge-wall sound.
- **White-browed robin-chat.** A crescendo song from riverine thickets at dawn and dusk; pairs duet ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-browed_robin-chat)).
- **Cicadas.** Only from Oct to Mar. *Platypleura* has a peak around 11 kHz ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platypleura_haglundi)). Local iNat cicada records fall in Sep to Nov.
- **The falls.** A broadband roar with strong low-frequency and infrasonic content, dominant at Boiling Pot and fading downstream. Lowest flow is Oct to Nov.

## What is built (October 2, 2026)

`ARaftSimWildlifeDirector` (`unreal/Source/SmokeEmIfYouGotEm/RaftSimWildlife.*`) is spawned by the game mode on every map. It stands down where `GetRiverWildlife` has no species table.

**How animals appear**
- Every 1.2 s it considers one species of the river's table. If fewer than that species' limit are about, it offers a sighting at the species' spawn chance; rare animals have low chances.
- It walks the current 55 to 220 m downstream from the raft, so spots stay on the river through bends, and places the animal in its habitat:
  - **Sky:** soaring over the river at the species' altitude.
  - **Canopy:** at the river-facing edge of the crown of the first bank trees. Trees are any tall instanced meshes, found by spatial query.
  - **Bank, shore and cliff:** found from the water's edge by line traces. Waders stand in the shallows where the bank rises steeply.
  - **Pool and rapid:** calm or fast water from the solver.
  - **Rock:** the tops of the rapids' boulder actors.
- Animals are removed once well behind the boat.

**How they look and move**
- Bodies are low-poly shaded primitives built procedurally: soaring and flapping birds, waders, ducks, a monkey, perched birds, quadrupeds, crocodile, salmon (and a hippo model, unused).
- Each kind has its own behaviour:
  - Soarers circle, bank, and flap only in short bursts. Condors barely flap.
  - Herons and ducks flush ahead of the boat; the heron croaks as it goes.
  - Ducks paddle and dive.
  - Quadrupeds walk, graze, and follow the ground.
  - Baboons bark at a passing boat.
  - Crocodiles slide off their rock.
  - Salmon leap with a splash.

**How they sound**
- Calls are synthesized by `RaftSimWildlife::FCallVoice` (`RaftSimAudio`). Each call is built from notes: whistles and chirps for birds, and band-limited buzz through formants for roars, croaks and barks.
- The figures above set each call, for example the howler at F0 112 Hz with formants at 413, 1037 and 1661 Hz, and the canyon wren's 5-6 kHz to 2 kHz cascade.
- Calls play from the animal with distance attenuation set by how far each carries: the howler 2 km, the eagle about 350 m.
- Heard-only species (canyon wren, hawks, woodpeckers, kingfishers, starlings, chucao, cicadas) play from cliffs, trees or the water's edge.

**Per river**, following the verdicts above:
- **South Fork:** bald eagle, turkey vulture, common merganser, great blue heron; heard: red-shouldered hawk, acorn woodpecker.
- **Hance:** California condor, desert bighorn (with horn clashes), raven, great blue heron; heard: canyon wren, cicadas.
- **Pacuare:** mantled howler, Montezuma oropendola, keel-billed toucan, sunbittern, fasciated tiger-heron; heard: ringed kingfisher, cicadas.
- **Futaleufú:** Andean condor, black-faced ibis, southern lapwing, austral parakeets, and rarely a torrent-duck pair; heard: chucao, ringed kingfisher.
- **Chilko:** sockeye leaping in the wall eddies, bald eagle, osprey, a merganser with ducklings, raven, and rarely a grizzly; heard: belted kingfisher.
- **Zambezi (both maps):** chacma baboons, Verreaux's eagle, rock pratincoles on midstream rocks, a juvenile crocodile, rarely a fish eagle (whose call is a duet), trumpeter hornbills; heard: red-winged starlings, cicadas. No hippos.

**Review and tests**
- `RaftSim.Wildlife.CallsAndRivers` renders every call to `Saved/RaftSimValidation/audio/wildlife/*.wav`. It checks that each call is audible and unclipped and sits in its register, that each river map has its animals, and that the Batoka has no hippos.
- `unreal/Scripts/review_river_wildlife.py` renders every model.
- In a running map, `RaftSim.WildlifeShowcase <label> [delaySeconds] [exit]` brings out each species near the raft and photographs it into `Saved/Screenshots`.

**Not modelled yet:** time of day and season, such as Pacuare cicadas silencing birds mid-morning, the Chilko salmon run in August, and Zambezi cicadas only from October to March. Also not yet: perched eagles on snags, flocks with real flocking, and ambience-only frogs.

## Surprises worth designing around

- **No hippos on the Batoka rapids.** The gorge's iconic animals are cliff-dwellers: baboons, black eagles, and rock pratincoles sitting on rocks in the rapids. Taita falcons, once the gorge's famous bird, were not found in the latest survey.
- **The Futaleufú is a poor bet for torrent ducks.** There are no main-stem records; they stay on tributaries.
- **Both condors are silent.** Their realism is in the flight: near-zero flapping and long soaring.
- **The real bald eagle call is a thin 3 to 5 kHz piping chatter.** Never use the red-tailed-hawk scream.
- **On the Pacuare, keel-billed toucan is recorded more than the chestnut-mandibled form outfitters advertise.** Scarlet macaws are absent; great green macaw is the plausible macaw.
- **Pacuare cicadas suppress bird and frog calling once they start in mid-morning.** This is a useful soundscape state change.
- **The Chilko's famous bear-and-eagle spectacle is at the lake outlet in mid-September, after the main rafting season.** In Lava Canyon, schooling salmon in late August are the reliable wildlife moment.
