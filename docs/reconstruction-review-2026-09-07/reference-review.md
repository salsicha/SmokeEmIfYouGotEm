Reviewed 2026-09-06 local time (2026-09-07 UTC). Reference interpretation only; no new surveyed boulder coordinates or rapid-identity acceptance is implied.

The [county river-map overview](https://www.eldoradocounty.ca.gov/Land-Use/Parks-Trails/Rivers/River-Maps) describes roughly 20 miles from Chili Bar to Salmon Falls. This supports continuing the captured-data route audit instead of keeping the old roughly 49 km scene merely because it renders.

[American River Resort](https://www.americanriverresort.com/adventures/whitewater-rafting-on-the-american-river/troublemaker) places the rapid beside its property and describes a boulder approach, drop and constrained S-shaped channel. Its description identifies prominent rock controls, not measured underwater geometry.

The [GoRafting rapid description](https://gorafting.com/united-states/california/south-fork-american/troublemaker/) independently describes the entrance islands, hole, rightward turn away from the left wall, and following drop beside a large rock. Its account is flow-dependent and remains reference-only; no commercial guide imagery or dataset was imported into game assets.

This agrees qualitatively with the user's requested hole/right/left sequence, but does not calibrate dimensions, flow, individual rock outlines or the inferred bed. Original NAIP imagery and LiDAR-return registration remain the candidate's geometric sources. ## September 7 browser frame review

The earlier retrieval failure is superseded for this video: the public YouTube
player worked in the browser. Inspected paused frames at 5-second intervals
from 0:05 through 0:55. The title card at 0:05 dates the trip June 24, 2016.
At 0:15–0:30, exposed shelves divide dark approach water with scattered narrow
breaking crests. At 0:35, the constricted drop is ahead. At 0:40–0:45, white
breaking water surrounds dark, steep faces and irregular troughs. At 0:45–0:50,
the raft passes a prominent rocky wall/control on the right; by 0:55, parked red
kayaks are visible on the left beside the opening runout.

These observations are consistent with the requested localized drop and
rock-constrained turns, not periodic waves across the whole channel. They are
not a measured turn-angle, boulder-coordinate, wave-height or recirculation
velocity survey. The moving camera and passengers obscure part of the surface.
Playback was interrupted by advertisements; do not treat the sparse frame review
as a continuous-motion or optical-flow measurement. No remote video was
downloaded or copied into game assets.

Compared these frames with `../reconstruction-review-2026-09-06/troublemaker_crux_registration.png`:
the 2022 NAIP image contains the exposed rock constriction and curved wet corridor,
whereas the hydro-flattened DEM masks the submerged rock controls. Their
qualitative resemblance is useful, but the six-year acquisition difference and
unknown video discharge prevent bathymetry or absolute registration acceptance.
The next registration check should use a bank-view reference to identify fixed
rock silhouettes against original LiDAR, not move measured rocks to fit one
uncalibrated camera view. Existing candidate geography remains unpromoted.

Also inspected [Qweniden's bank-side Troublemaker recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2XTbOCNDcZQ)
at 0:58 and 1:03. Its title dates the trip July 15, 2022, six days before the
NAIP acquisition. The side view shows angular shelves with narrow overfalls,
white collapsing crests over darker water faces, and a prominent near-vertical
rock wall beside the downstream channel. Camera panning means this is not a
fixed photogrammetric camera. It is a better exposed-rock reference than a
moving raft camera, but has no calibration/discharge metadata. No geometry
coordinates or game acceptance status were changed from these two frames.
