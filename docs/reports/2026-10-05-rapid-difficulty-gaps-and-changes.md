# Why the rapids play easy, and what to change

Follow-up to [the rapid difficulty assessment](2026-10-05-rapid-difficulty-assessment.md).
In that run, 58 of the 70 graded rapid sections went through clean with no
commands at all, and 66 of the 70 played easier than catalogued. That
includes rapids catalogued IV–V and V. This report
does three things: summarises what makes each real rapid hard, from guidebooks,
outfitters, American Whitewater accident reports and trip reports; explains why
the game's water and raft cannot produce those hazards (with code references);
and proposes changes in priority order.

## What went wrong, in one paragraph

Real rapids punish a boat that does nothing. Across all six rivers the sources
describe the same pattern: the main current aims at something (a cliff, a rock,
a hole, a lateral wave), and a raft that drifts or loses its angle hits it
sideways, then flips, pins, or loses people. The game does the opposite.

- **The water is too smooth.** It is a depth-averaged shallow-water solution on
  1–10 m cells, with the feature forcing that could add holes and laterals
  switched off. The surface the raft rides is capped at about 0.4 m of relief.
- **The rocks are wrong.** They are low mounds under the surface, and the hull
  slides off them without friction.
- **The raft cannot be tripped.** It is very stiff in roll and is dragged to the
  speed of the water within a fraction of a second, so it behaves like a cork
  following the streamlines around every hazard. Current pushes on it at the
  middle of the tubes, so it can never trip it over, and it only capsizes once
  it is physically upside down.

The only places that bite are the steep drops at Pacuare's Huacas and the
Futaleufú's Terminator, where the cooked water itself contains a multi-metre
surge.

## What makes each rapid hard

The full research, with sources, is summarised per river below. "Passive boat"
is what the sources say happens to a raft that drifts with no input. "Game" is
the class it played in the assessment.

### South Fork American (~1,600 cfs)

At this flow the South Fork is technical, not big. The danger is rocks and
placement: the main current slams into Racehorse Bend's right cliff, Rhino Rock,
House Rock, Satan's left wall and Bouncing Rock. Barely covered rocks wrap rafts:
Death Star, Frog Rock, Gunsight, House Rock's boulder, the bottom rock at Scissors
and Catcher's Mitt. Diagonals flip a raft that hits them at an angle:
Troublemaker, the Racehorse wall lateral, Satan's shelf lateral and curler, and
Hospital Bar. Swims are rough, over shallow cracked rock and through back-to-back
rapids.

### Colorado, Hance (8,000 cfs, oar rig)

At 8k cfs Hance is a forced, timed move across a rocky debris fan, not a big-wave
rapid. Since the 2012 Red Canyon debris flow the run is right-to-left. The rower
enters on the right and must immediately back-ferry hard left past **Emilio's
Hole**, a keeper at low water, putting the stern into the Duck Pond. Otherwise
the current carries the boat right into the pour-overs and the **Land of the
Giants** boulder island with its stacked holes. A few seconds late means
sideways into Emilio's Hole and a flip (2022 AW report). There have been pins
lasting hours, boats surfed for 25 minutes and fatal flips near 8k. Son of
Hance (III) has a hidden centre-right hole and whirlpool eddy fences that held a
Hance swimmer under in 2022.

|---|---|---|---|
| Hance | 8/10 (IV+) | Into Emilio's Hole or the Giants: held, flipped or pinned | I |
| Son of Hance | III | Hole; swimmers caught in whirlpools | I |

## Why the game cannot produce these hazards

Code references are relative to the repository.

**1. The water model smooths out every feature that makes a rapid hard.**
- The river is a 2-D depth-averaged shallow-water solve (HLL, MUSCL) with
  Manning friction and no turbulence or non-hydrostatic terms
  (`unreal/Plugins/SEIYGECore/cpp/include/raftsim_water/solver.hpp`). A hole can only appear as a
  step in depth with downstream flow: no surface reversal, no breaking volume,
  no boils.
- Cells are 1–2 m on most maps and 5×10 m on the full Zambezi. The bed is
  resampled by averaging, which rounds features further
  (`physics/scripts/build_curvilinear_river_scenario.py`).
- The solver already has forcing for holes, laterals, boils, wave trains and
  ledges (`unreal/Plugins/SEIYGECore/cpp/src/solver_runtime.cpp`). Every map ships
  `feature_strength_scale: 0.0`, and the live and Cartesian windows reject any
  other value (`RaftSimWater/Private/RaftSimLiveWaterWindow.cpp`,
  `unreal/Plugins/SEIYGECore/cpp/src/cartesian_domain.cpp`).
- The surface the raft rides is capped:
  - hydraulic relief at most 0.40 m;
  - rapid-grade waves at most 0.18 m;
  - breaking-crest lift at most 0.25 m;
  - ±3 m smoothing on top of that.

  See `RaftSimWater/Private/RaftSimWaterRuntimeAdapter.cpp` and
  `RaftSimRaft/Public/RaftSimWaterSurfaceActor.h`. A 1.2 m "physical crest"
  model exists, but it is gated on a Chilko path string that the Lava Canyon
  map does not use, so it is probably off everywhere.
- Authored hole currents only reverse the surface at very high upstream Froude
  numbers. Eddies need an owner, and the Chilko, Terminator, Batoka and Upper
  Gorge windows have none (`docs/plans/remaining-work.md`).
- `docs/release/known-issues.md` already records the symptom on Meat Grinder:
  a re-cooked 0.7 m jump rolls a broadside raft 31° and carries it through in
  under two seconds.

**2. The rocks are not there, or are not solid.**
- Pacuare, Terminator and the Zambezi beds come from 10–30 m DEMs with no
  bathymetry. Boulders are inferred mounds of 1–2.5 m radius whose crests sit
  0.3–0.4 m *below* the surface. Bank rocks are visual only.
- The hull's contact with the bed is normal-only, with no friction
  (`RaftSimPhysics/Private/RaftSimHullContact.cpp`), so it slides off.
- The pin/wrap model only works with rock obstacle actors, and only the South
  Fork map spawns them. They are infinite vertical cylinders with no pillow-rock
  lift, and being "pinned" only drives damage. There is no hold.

**3. The raft is a cork.**
- Loaded mass is 605 kg. Roll stiffness is about 0.78 kN·m per degree, and the
  buoyancy reserve has a floor of 5.2 × weight.
- Gravity acts at the chamber-centre plane, and current drag acts at the
  chamber centres. On a level raft, a cross-current only yaws it; nothing can
  trip it (`RaftSimRaft/Private/RaftSimRaftActor.cpp`,
  `RaftSimPhysics/.../RaftSimFlexibleRaftModel.cpp`).
- The hull drag floor matches the raft to the current within about 0.2–1 s.
  The raft follows streamlines around obstacles, and every load that scales
  with slip speed stays small.
- Angular damping is 1.4 /s, on top of implicit drag damping.
- It capsizes only once it is physically past 100° of roll. The flip-risk latch
  is telemetry, and `CapsizeLatchSeconds` and `CapsizeTransitionSeconds` are
  unused. The flip lab needed a 3.2 m broadside crest to flip it
  (`docs/boat-flip-lab/README.md`).

**4. The hazard rules only fire in the two surges.**
- Washout needs water more than 0.15 m above the hip moving at least 2 m/s
  relative to the boat.
- The new swamped-seat rule needs a seat 0.5 m under the surface for 0.6 s.

With a raft that matches the current and a surface capped at 0.4 m, neither
happens except at Huacas and Terminator.

**5. Three map coverage problems** found while playing:
- **The Wall** is upstream of the Upper Gorge put-in.
- **Guatemala's** section is clipped by the end of the reach.
- **Lower Huacas'** surge sits across every line, where it should only punish
  boats that drift left onto the undercut wall.

## Proposed changes, in priority order

Each step should be checked by re-running the assessment
(`unreal/Scripts/run_rapid_assessment.ps1`). The target is that hands-off runs
fail on everything catalogued III or above, and that IV–V rapids have narrow
clean lines and missed turns that cost swimmers or flips.

### 1. Let the raft be tripped (small code changes, biggest effect)

1. **Apply current drag where the water acts:** at the tube bottoms, below the
   centre of mass, instead of at the chamber centres. A cross-current then rolls
   the raft toward the downstream tube, which is exactly why guides high-side.
   High-siding becomes a real counter instead of a gesture.
2. **Put the crew and centre of gravity above the waterline**, so weight on a
   heeled raft adds roll moment, and crew shifts change the real moment instead
   of just a threshold multiplier.
3. **Make capsize happen before full inversion.** Use the existing flip-risk
   latch (`CapsizeLatchSeconds`, `CapsizeTransitionSeconds`): when the D3 flip
   margin is exceeded for about 0.35 s, start the capsize transition.
4. **Soften roll:** lower the buoyancy-reserve floor (5.2 → about 3 × weight)
   and angular damping (1.4 → about 0.6 /s). Re-run the flip lab so that a
   2–2.5 m broadside breaking crest flips an unweighted raft.
5. **Lower the hull-drag floor** so the raft keeps some slip relative to the
   water. Holes and laterals then load it, and the washout rule (which needs
   relative flow) can fire.

### 2. Put real features in the water (tuning plus per-rapid authoring)

6. **Turn feature forcing on.** Allow `feature_strength_scale > 0` in the live
   and Cartesian windows. Author per-rapid `hole`, `lateral`, `boil`,
   `wave_train` and `ledge` features at the stations the research gives, sized
   from the catalogue: Himalayas ~4.5 m waves, The Mother ~4 m, Oblivion's third
   wave 4–5 m, Bidwell 1.5–3 m, Star Trek up to 5 m.
7. **Raise the surface caps** for features that warrant it: hydraulic relief
   (0.4 m), breaking-crest lift (0.25 m) and rapid-grade waves (0.18 m). Fix the
   physical-crest path check so the 1.2 m crest model runs on every map.
8. **Holes that hold:** lower the threshold for surface reversal in the
   hole-current model and widen its lane, so named keeper holes recirculate.
   Named holes include Emilio's Hole, Terminator Hole, China Hole, Catcher's
   Mitt, the Washing Machine, Double Trouble, Star Trek/Muncher, Troublemaker's
   gut and First Threat.
9. **Laterals that turn the boat:** Troublemaker's diagonal, the Racehorse and
   Satan's wall laterals, Bidwell's entry lateral, Directors Wave, Morning
   Glory's wall diagonals and Hospital Bar.
10. **Boils, whirlpools and eddy lines** for big water (Zambezi 1, 6, 11, 12C, 13,
    15, 23; Son of Hance), with eddy owners on the Batoka, Upper Gorge,
    Terminator and Chilko maps.

### 3. Put solid rocks where people pin

11. **Spawn pin rocks on every map** at the catalogued rocks:
    - South Fork: Death Star, Rhino, Frog Rock, Gunsight, House Rock, the
      Scissors bottom rock, Bouncing Rock, Catcher's Mitt.
    - Hance: the entrance boulder and the Land of the Giants.
    - Pacuare: the Upper Huacas boulder and the Lower Pinball rocks.
    - Chilko: Bidwell's flat boulder and the White Mile slab.
    - Futaleufú: the Terminator Entrance boulders.
12. **Make pins real:** add tangential friction to hull contact, a vertical
    (pillow) component to the rock normal, and a hold constraint while pinned,
    released by high-side or pulling free.
13. **Re-cook with emergent features:** imprint taller and surfacing boulders
    and ledges from the observed-rapid files (`imprint_observed_rapids.py
    --surface-from`) at 1 m. Meat Grinder is the precedent.

### 4. Fix the maps and the data

14. Move the Upper Gorge put-in above The Wall (190 m), or drop The Wall from
    that map.
15. Extend Guatemala's section past its hole, or end the reach after it.
16. Make Lower Huacas position-dependent: keep the surge at the left wall and
    open the right-working line.
17. Better terrain where the sources show the hazards are rocks: Pacuare and
    Terminator DEMs, and the Batoka high-resolution request.
18. Offer more flow bands. Several rapids change character with level (Maya,
    Meat Grinder, Hance, Bidwell, White Mile), and the full Zambezi's discharge
    is uncalibrated.

### Washout tuning (after 1–3)

Re-check the washout and swamped-seat thresholds once the water has real
breaking crests and the raft has slip. Then a broadside hit in a big wave should
throw paddlers, as the Zambezi and Futaleufú reports describe, while a square
hit with a braced crew mostly keeps them in.
